Separate dispute terms

Proposed Mutual Dispute Resolution Agreement

Customer service first. Arbitration for covered disputes if a finalized agreement is separately accepted.

Review draft — not currently effective

Version November 8, 2026. This proposed agreement is between Apex Code Works California and the customer identified in a future signed service or payment-plan agreement. It requires California legal review and separate, affirmative acceptance before use. Visiting this page or acknowledging this draft in the application-instructions form does not activate arbitration or waive court rights. The proposed terms below describe how a finalized agreement would operate.

Customer service and voluntary settlement

Either party would first send a written description of the dispute and requested resolution to the other. Customers may contact Raymond.Angel.Developer@gmail.com; the company would use the customer contact in the service agreement. The parties would have 30 calendar days after receipt to try to resolve the matter. Neither party would have to accept a settlement. This process would not prevent a timely filing needed to preserve a claim or obtain urgent relief.

Mutual binding arbitration and trial waiver

IF SEPARATELY ACCEPTED, THIS AGREEMENT WOULD REQUIRE BOTH PARTIES TO RESOLVE COVERED DISPUTES THROUGH BINDING ARBITRATION INSTEAD OF A COURT TRIAL BEFORE A JUDGE OR JURY. COURT REVIEW OF AN ARBITRATION AWARD IS LIMITED. THE EXCEPTIONS BELOW REMAIN.

Unresolved disputes concerning the specified website project, care services, or installment agreement would go to one neutral arbitrator administered by JAMS under its Streamlined Arbitration Rules and Procedures, subject to mandatory law and any applicable JAMS consumer standards. Both parties would participate in selecting the arbitrator. No dispute would be decided by the company acting as arbitrator. A court, rather than the arbitrator, would decide whether this arbitration agreement was formed and is enforceable.

Court and statutory rights preserved

Either party could bring an eligible claim in small-claims court. This proposal would not bar regulatory complaints, government investigations, nonwaivable public injunctive relief, or claims that law does not permit to be forced into arbitration. A court could provide permissible provisional relief, address enforcement of this agreement, or confirm or review an award as the law allows. There is no separate jury waiver for claims remaining in court, no class-action waiver in this draft, and no requirement to keep a complaint from regulators.

Location, procedure, remedies, and costs

Hearings would be remote if both parties agree, or at a reasonably accessible location. Relevant evidence could be exchanged, each party could retain counsel, and the arbitrator would issue a reasoned written decision. Applicable statutory remedies would remain available. JAMS rules, required fee allocations, and available fee waivers would govern. A customer would not automatically owe the company's legal fees for losing. The final agreement must specify commercial-case fees and any additional customer fee protections before acceptance; consumer standards do not automatically apply to business-purpose purchases.

Opt-out and changes

The proposed final agreement would allow either party to opt out of arbitration by written notice within 30 calendar days after its separate acceptance. A customer could email the business with their name, project reference, and a clear opt-out statement. Opting out would not change the agreed service price or credit-eligibility criteria. The company could not retroactively change dispute terms for an existing dispute. If JAMS could not administer the agreed procedure and no lawful alternative were mutually agreed, either party could pursue an appropriate court remedy.

Finalization and separate acceptance

Before activation, the final document must identify the legal contracting parties, covered service agreement, applicable law, arbitration rules and fees, contact details, and effective version. Each party must receive a retainable copy. The eventual acceptance record should capture the agreement version, affirmative choice, customer identity, and acceptance date. This preliminary request form records only acknowledgment of the draft. It does not collect an arbitration signature or establish enforceability.

Drafting references

Based on the structure of the JAMS Clause Workbook and informed by JAMS Consumer Minimum Standards. These are provider references, not a California-approved template or legal opinion. This draft does not represent JAMS approval, registration, or a provider partnership.
